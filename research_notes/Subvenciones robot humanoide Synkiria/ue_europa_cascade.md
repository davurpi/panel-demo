# EU non-repayable funding for Synkiria Physical AI (humanoid Unitree R1 EDU): cascade/FSTP open calls, EDIH, EIC, Eurostars

Research date: 18 October 2026. Every amount and date below comes from the cited source. Text marked "Assessment" is the researcher's own judgement, not a sourced fact. Aggregator sources (cascadefunding.eu, fundsforngos, subsdy, grantedai) were used to find calls. Wherever possible the official call URL is given and should be checked before applying.

## 1. Which cascade-funding / FSTP robotics & AI open calls are OPEN on 18-Oct-2026?

### Takeaway
I found only one EU-funded robotics FSTP call that is clearly open on 18 October 2026: **FORTIS Open Call #2** (human-robot interaction), which closes on **4 November 2026**. It needs a consortium, and SMEs are funded at 70%, not 100%. The other calls checked (ARISE, JARVIS, euROBIN, ROB4GREEN, AI-BOOST, AID4SME, DeployAI/AIoD) had closed by October 2026. These are the best calls to track for the next editions.

### Cited Findings
**FORTIS Open Call #2 (Horizon Europe project FORTIS): OPEN**
- Submission runs 1 Sep 2026 to **4 Nov 2026, 17:00 Brussels**. Results are expected on 14 Dec 2026. Official page: https://fortis-project.eu/open-call-2/; applications go through F6S (https://www.f6s.com/fortis-open-call-2/apply). — [cascadefunding.eu analysis](https://cascadefunding.eu/fortis-open-call-analysis-hri-robotics-solutions/); [fundsforNGOs](https://www2.fundsforngos.org/innovation/call-for-applications-fortis-open-call-2/)
- Maximum funding is €250,000 per project under OC#2.1 and €170,000 under OC#2.2. The total budget is about €3.14M, and at least 8 + 6 projects will be funded. — [cascadefunding.eu](https://cascadefunding.eu/fortis-open-call-analysis-hri-robotics-solutions/)
- Funding rate: **100% for non-profits, 70% for SMEs and other for-profit entities**. Funding is a lump sum paid 30% / 30% / 40% after three sprints of 3, 3 and 4 months, each released after a positive review. **No prefinancing is mentioned.** — [cascadefunding.eu](https://cascadefunding.eu/fortis-open-call-analysis-hri-robotics-solutions/)
- Consortium rules: OC#2.1 needs at least 3 entities from at least 2 eligible countries. OC#2.2 needs at least 2 entities and can be single-country. Every consortium needs at least 1 SME, and non-industry partners may take at most 30% of the budget. SMEs are eligible if their core business involves HRI technology. Projects must enter at TRL 4–5 and reach TRL 7. OC#2.2 is an open topic that must integrate at least 2 FORTIS ROS packages in a civilian use case. At least 50% of OC#2.2 funding goes to agriculture, mining, forestry and fishing; other eligible sectors include healthcare, events, airports and shopping malls. Construction, manufacturing logistics and railway maintenance are excluded. — [cascadefunding.eu](https://cascadefunding.eu/fortis-open-call-analysis-hri-robotics-solutions/)

**SYSTEMEU FSTP open call**
- Deadline 16 Nov 2026, 17:00 Brussels. The search results did not show a robotics/AI scope, so this has not been verified. — [systemeuvalley.eu](https://systemeuvalley.eu/open-call-for-fstp/)

**NEURA Robotics "MiPA" Physical AI Research Programme. Note: private company programme, NOT EU funding.**
- Deadline 30 Oct 2026, 24:00 CET. Up to €500,000 per project, open to universities, institutes and startups. — [neura-robotics.com](https://neura-robotics.com/mipa-research-program/)

### Inferences
- Assessment: FORTIS #2 is a poor fit for Synkiria on its own. It needs ROS-based HRI tech at TRL 4–5 built on FORTIS packages, plus a partner (and for OC#2.1, a foreign partner). Synkiria would also have to cofinance 30%, and the sector focus (agri, mining, forestry and fishing for at least 50% of OC#2.2) is far from reception and ERP use. A proposal is only realistic if Synkiria joins an existing consortium in the next 2 weeks. Low probability.
- Assessment: the NEURA MiPA programme is aimed at universities, institutes and startups, uses NEURA's own platform, and is not EU money. It is a low fit.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm FORTIS eligible-cost rules, for example whether hardware purchase counts. The Guidelines for Applicants were not read.
- I could not do a full live scan of the F&T portal list "Calls for proposals from third parties" for 18 Oct 2026 within the time limit. Other open calls may exist.

### Inferences
- Assessment: **JARVIS-type HRI calls are the best fit** for Synkiria. A single SME can apply, the lump sum is €100k, and the subject is HRI. They have two weaknesses: they require ROS2, and their use cases are industrial. A Unitree R1 EDU supports ROS2, but Synkiria would need ROS2 skills.
- Assessment: for the next 6–12 months the realistic plan is to subscribe to F6S, cascadefunding.eu and the EU-Robotics newsletters, and to be ready to apply when the next HRI, embodied-AI or GenAI-robotics call opens. Synkiria could also join a Catalan RTO such as Eurecat as an end user or use case in a consortium.
- Assessment: FSTP lump sums are paid against deliverables, not invoices. In practice the money can be spent on hardware such as the robot, provided the call guidelines allow it. JARVIS Track 2 requires "own robotic platform", which means the hardware is expected to exist already.
- No calls specific to humanoid robots were found. The search returned no FSTP call targeting humanoids. — [cascadefunding.eu search results](https://cascadefunding.eu/cascade-funding-march-2026/)

## 5. EIC Accelerator (grant + optional equity)

### Takeaway
The EIC Accelerator is a single-SME instrument. It offers a **grant below €2.5M for TRL 6–8** work plus optional equity of €1–10M. Short proposals can be submitted at any time. The last 2026 full-proposal cut-off is **4 November 2026**, and the 2027 dates had not been checked. Assessment: Synkiria's fit is weak. It is integrating third-party technology (a Unitree robot plus Claude), which is not deep-tech breakthrough innovation, and the jury process is highly competitive.

### Cited Findings
- Grant: a lump sum "below EUR 2.5 million" for TRL 6–8 work over up to 24 months. Investment (equity/quasi-equity): €1–10M. — [EIC official](https://eic.ec.europa.eu/eic-funding-opportunities/eic-accelerator_en)
- Eligible applicants: single start-ups and SMEs in the EU or associated countries. Only one application per applicant at a time. — [EIC official](https://eic.ec.europa.eu/eic-funding-opportunities/eic-accelerator_en)
- Process: a short proposal can be submitted at any time and is batched on the first Tuesday of each month, with feedback in about 4–6 weeks. The full proposal requires a 20-page form, pitch deck, Letters of Intent, an FTO analysis and a video; results take 8–9 weeks. The process ends with a jury interview, and investment decisions take 2–6 months. — [EIC official](https://eic.ec.europa.eu/eic-funding-opportunities/eic-accelerator_en)
- 2026 full-proposal cut-offs: 7 Jan, 4 Mar, 6 May, 8 Jul, 2 Sep, **4 Nov 2026**. — [EIC official](https://eic.ec.europa.eu/eic-funding-opportunities/eic-accelerator_en); confirmed by [Hezelburcht](https://www.hezelburcht.com/en/news/eic-launches-2026-work-programme-an-overview-of-the-eic-instruments-for-2026/). EU for Digital gives "4 October" as the last date, which conflicts with these sources. — [eufordigital](https://eufordigital.eu/horizon-europe-eu-simplifies-application-process-for-eic-accelerator/)
- 2026 budget: €414M for Open and €220M for Challenges. — [EIC official](https://eic.ec.europa.eu/eic-funding-opportunities/eic-accelerator_en)

### Inferences
- Assessment: in practice the 4 Nov 2026 cut-off cannot be reached. A short proposal would first need approval, which takes about 4–6 weeks. The first realistic target is a 2027 cut-off.
- Assessment: the funding rate for EIC grant-only awards is commonly cited as 70% of eligible costs, but this was not stated on the official page I read, so it is unverified. The EIC also expects high-risk breakthrough innovation with large market and scale-up ambitions. An Odoo integrator with €290k turnover deploying an off-the-shelf humanoid would struggle to pass the jury. **Fit: low.**

### Gaps
- Success rates were not published on the page I read. The 2027 cut-off dates and the confirmed funding rate (%) are also missing.

## 7. Equipment / hardware eligibility under Horizon Europe lump sums

### Takeaway
In Horizon Europe actual-cost and lump-sum budgets, **equipment is by default eligible only as depreciation**. Full cost is eligible only where the call conditions allow it. In FSTP calls the project's guidelines set the rules, and payment is made against deliverables.

### Cited Findings
- Depreciation costs are eligible by default, and full costs only by exception. "Full costs are eligible only if allowed by the call conditions." — [ANI (Portuguese NCP) "How to Manage your HE Grant" 2026](https://ani.pt/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/How-to-Manage-your-HE-Grant.pdf)
- For lump-sum calls, the cost estimate must include only costs eligible under actual-cost rules, and the depreciable amount is spread over the asset's useful life. — [ERJU Call 2022 Q&A](https://rail-research.europa.eu/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/QACall2022_1stPublication.pdf)

### Inferences
- Assessment: in a direct Horizon Europe or EIC proposal, the robot's cost (Unitree R1 EDU) would usually count only as depreciation for the project period, unless the call allows full cost. In FSTP calls, the third party receives a lump sum against milestones. The project's guidelines decide whether costs are checked, so the FORTIS, JARVIS and ARISE guidelines must be read for each call.

### Gaps
- I did not read the actual FSTP guideline clauses on hardware for FORTIS, JARVIS or ARISE.
